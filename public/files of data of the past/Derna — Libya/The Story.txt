1. Derna, Libya — The Catastrophic Dam Failures (2023)
The Historical Context & Narrative
Located on the northeastern coast of Libya, Derna was historically known as the "Pearl of the Mediterranean" due to its lush greenery, waterfalls, and citrus orchards fed by the seasonal Wadi Derna river. The city sits at the mouth of a steep, narrow canyon cutting down from the Jabal al-Akhdar (Green Mountain) plateau. Because of its topography, Derna had a long history of flash flooding, which prompted the construction of two major flood-control dams in the 1970s by a Yugoslavian engineering firm: the upper Bu Mansour Dam (13 km upstream) and the lower Derna Dam (just 1 km from the city's edge).

On the night of September 10, 2023, Storm Daniel—a rare, slow-moving Mediterranean tropical-like cyclone (medicane)—stalled directly over northeastern Libya. In less than 24 hours, the region received over 400 millimeters of rain, an astronomical volume for an arid climate that normally sees less than 300 millimeters over an entire year. Residents initially heard loud explosions in the dead of night, mistaking them for artillery fire, before a wall of mud, boulders, and debris up to 7 meters (23 feet) high tore through the sleeping city. Within minutes, roughly one-quarter of Derna was completely erased from the map, sweeping thousands of people and entire multi-story residential blocks out into the Mediterranean Sea.

Geotechnical & Hydrological Causes
Cascading Dam Failure (The Domino Effect): The upper Bu Mansour Dam was designed to hold roughly 22.5 million cubic meters of water, while the lower Derna Dam had a much smaller capacity of 1.5 million cubic meters. When the torrential runoff overwhelmed and breached the upper dam first, it released a massive wave of water and mud directly onto the smaller lower dam, causing it to burst instantaneously.

Neglected Maintenance & Material Fatigue: Both dams were constructed using clay-core embankment designs rather than solid reinforced concrete. Due to over a decade of political instability and civil conflict in Libya, routine maintenance, sediment dredging, and structural inspections had been completely neglected. Cracks documented in hydraulic reports as early as 1998 had never been repaired.

Urban Encroachment & Channel Constriction: Over the decades, unchecked urban expansion pushed residential neighborhoods directly into the natural floodplain of Wadi Derna. Furthermore, bridges spanning the river channel inside the city acted as bottlenecks; as trees, vehicles, and rubble jammed against bridge pylons, water backed up before surging outward with exponentially greater destructive force.
