The Story

On December 26, 2004, a massive undersea earthquake off the coast of Sumatra triggered one of the deadliest tsunamis in modern history.

The coastal town of Lhoknga was almost completely destroyed as enormous waves swept inland, carrying away homes, vegetation, roads, and infrastructure. Remarkably, the central mosque remained standing while much of the surrounding town disappeared.

Satellite images captured the settlement before and after the disaster, revealing the extraordinary scale of destruction.

Main Causes
- A magnitude 9.1 undersea earthquake.
- Sudden displacement of the ocean floor.
- Powerful tsunami waves reaching the coast.
- Low-lying coastal geography.
